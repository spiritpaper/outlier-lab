# Why a video worked (or didn't)

Every view goes through the same steps:

> **YouTube shows the thumbnail** (impressions) → **someone clicks** (click-through rate) → **they keep watching** (retention, average view duration) → **they're glad they did** (likes, comments, shares, coming back)

YouTube watches all four, for every video, and shows the video to more people when viewers respond well. Its help page calls this content performance: "whether they choose to click, watch, or positively engage". So when a video underperforms, find the step where it lost people. Each step points to a different fix.

Your own numbers come from **My channel** (Google sign-in, see the [guide](../guides/my-channel.md)). For other channels you only have the public numbers: views, likes, comments and the outlier score.

## Find the weak step

| What you see | What it usually means | What to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Few impressions** | YouTube didn't show it much. The topic may not interest your audience, or early viewers didn't respond (low CTR or retention in the first hours). | Check the idea first: is it something your audience wants? Look at what outliers in your niche are about ([Content gaps](../guides/channels.md#content-gaps), [Keyword explorer](../guides/keyword-explorer.md)). |
| **Good impressions, low CTR** | People saw it and didn't click. The packaging didn't sell it, or the idea doesn't appeal. | Rework the title and thumbnail. If you're in the YouTube Partner Program, use **Test & compare** on YouTube Studio (up to three titles and thumbnails). |
| **High CTR, low retention** | The click worked, but the video didn't deliver what the title and thumbnail promised, or the opening was slow. | Look at the first 30 seconds in the retention curve. Does the opening confirm the promise right away? |
| **Good CTR and retention, few impressions** | Often a small, loyal audience: YouTube says it's "common for videos with fewer impressions and views to have higher click-through-rates and average view duration". | Wait a few weeks; good videos can be picked up later in Suggested and Search. Next time, choose an idea with wider appeal. |
| **CTR falls while impressions grow** | Normal. YouTube is now showing it on Home to people who know you less. | Nothing to fix. Look at views and watch time, not CTR alone. |
| **High CTR, low retention, few impressions** | YouTube's own example of what clickbait looks like. | Make the title and thumbnail honest about what the video is. |

## What's a "good" number?

There's no universal target. YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR **between 2% and 10%**, and that it varies with the type of content, the audience and where the thumbnail was shown. New videos, new channels and videos under 100 views vary even more.

**Compare yourself with yourself.** Your median is the benchmark that matters:

- The **outlier score** on My channel's top videos is against *your own* median, the same math as for every other channel.
- A project's **After publishing** section takes the score at **48 hours** and **7 days**, next to the outliers you modelled it on. Those two snapshots are fair comparisons between your videos.
- In retention, YouTube's **typical retention** compares a video with your last 10 videos of similar length.

## When to judge a video

- **The first 48 hours** tell you mostly how your subscribers and regular viewers responded.
- **7 days** is a fairer read: Browse and Suggested have had time to test it.
- **30 days** is the full picture for most videos. Outlier Lab tracks a linked project for 30 days, then moves it to the board's Archive with its final numbers.
- **Evergreen videos** (tutorials, searches people keep making) can keep growing for months. Look at **views per day now**: if it's steady or rising, the video isn't done.

YouTube's Analytics data lags by about 2 days, so the most recent days on My channel are always incomplete.

## Traffic sources tell you where it worked

| Source | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Browse features** (Home, Subscriptions) | YouTube recommended it on people's home pages. This is where most growth comes from, and it depends on the click and on how satisfied viewers are. |
| **Suggested videos** | Shown next to or after another video. YouTube says the video someone is watching is "the main signal" here. Watch which videos send you traffic: those are your neighbours. |
| **YouTube search** | People looked for it. Titles that match what people type matter here, and these videos often keep getting views for years. |
| **Shorts feed** | Shorts viewers swiping. A separate recommendation system from long-form. |
| **External** | Links from other sites and apps. Spikes here usually come from one share. |

My channel shows traffic sources with their change from the previous period. The AI review uses them too, so it can say, for example, that Search held steady while Browse fell.

## Do this after every upload

1. At 48 hours and 7 days, look at the outlier score in the project's **After publishing** section.
2. Find the weak step in the table above.
3. Open the retention curve and note the biggest drop (see [Reading retention curves](retention.md)).
4. Write one lesson in the [Lab notebook](../guides/lab-notebook.md): "Slow intro, 38% left by 0:30", "Title too vague".
5. If you ran a test screening in the [Audience lab](../guides/audience-lab.md), check **Forecast vs reality**: it compares the screening with what really happened and calibrates the next one.
