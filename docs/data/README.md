# Reading your data

Outlier Lab gives you a lot of numbers. This section is about what to do with them: how to tell a real outlier from a lucky one, why one of your videos worked and another didn't, what a retention curve is telling you, and whether your channel is actually growing.

## Three rules that apply everywhere

1. **Compare a video with its own channel, and with the same format.** 50K views is a breakout on one channel and a flop on another. That's why every score in Outlier Lab is against the channel's median, and Shorts are scored against Shorts.
2. **Look for patterns, not single numbers.** One outlier is a clue. The same idea working on two or three channels is evidence. One bad month is noise. Three falling blocks in a row is a trend.
3. **Wait for enough data.** A video under 3 days old hasn't collected its views. YouTube's own advice is to look at click-through rate only after "a substantial number of impressions", and to ignore small changes.
