# Reading an outlier score

An outlier score of **4.2x** means the video has 4.2 times the median views of its channel's 30 most recent videos of the same format. It tells you the video reached far beyond the people who usually watch that channel. Something about it (the idea, the title and thumbnail, the timing) pulled in viewers who wouldn't normally click.

That's what makes outliers useful: they show what works *beyond* a channel's fans, which is exactly what you need to grow.

## Is it a real outlier?

Before you learn from a high score, check these. Each one can inflate it.

| Check | Why it matters | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How many videos is the baseline based on?** | A channel with 8 videos has a shaky median. One of them doing well proves less. | The video panel shows the median and how many of the channel's videos it's based on. |
| **Is the channel growing fast?** | If the channel's subscribers doubled recently, its new videos all beat the old median. That's channel growth, not an outlier idea. | The **trend** in the video panel or in [Following](../guides/channels.md). A "growing" channel inflates scores. |
| **Is it news or a trend?** | A video about this week's event can be a 10x outlier and teach you nothing you can repeat next month. | The title, the upload date, and whether similar videos from the same week spiked too. |
| **Was it pushed from outside?** | A collab, a viral tweet or a shout-out from a bigger creator brings views the idea didn't earn. | The description and comments ("came from…"). |
| **Is it very new?** | Under 3 days, the score is still rising, so it may end up higher, or level off as typical. | The age in the panel. **Views per day now** shows whether it's still climbing. |
| **Does it straddle YouTube's view change?** | From 24 August 2026, YouTube counts a view from the first frame (including a video autoplaying on Home), where it used to wait a few seconds. Public counts for newer videos can run higher than older ones from the same channel. | The upload dates of the video and the channel's baseline. |

**The strongest signal:** the same idea, title structure or format is an outlier on **two or more channels**. That's why [Content gaps](../guides/channels.md#content-gaps) only counts topics found in 2+ outliers (or one 5x+ breakout), and why the keyword explorer's **Small-channel hunt** preset looks for outliers on channels under 25K subscribers. When a small channel breaks out, it's the idea that worked, not the channel's fame.

## What to study

Once you trust it, take the outlier apart. Save it (☆ or **Save idea**) so it's re-checked daily, then go through these:

1. **The idea.** Say it in one sentence without the title. Would your audience care? Paddy Galloway, who has worked with MrBeast and Ryan Trahan, says an idea should appeal to your core viewers *and* to casual and new ones.
2. **The title.** What structure is it? A number, a time span, an experiment ("I tried…"), a comparison, a warning, a question. The [Title lab](../guides/scripts-and-titles.md) finds which proven outliers your own title is closest to.
3. **The thumbnail.** What is it *showing*: a situation, an object, a face, a before and after? How many words are on it? Does it add something the title doesn't say?
4. **The first 30 seconds.** Open the **Transcript** and run **Analyze script**. Does it repeat the title's promise? Open a question? Raise stakes? The hook signals are listed in the analysis.
5. **Where people rewatch.** The **Most replayed** heatmap in the video panel shows the moments viewers went back to. Those are the parts worth borrowing.
6. **What people asked for.** **Comments** pulls out requests and questions. A "part 2" request on a 5x outlier is a ready-made idea.
7. **The length and the format.** Is it a 9-minute story or a 40-minute deep dive? A Short? Copy the idea, not necessarily the length, but notice it.

Write what you learn in the [Lab notebook](../guides/lab-notebook.md): it brings the right lesson back while you're writing your next script.

## From outlier to your own idea

Don't copy the video. Copy what made it work, and change the rest:

- **Same idea, your angle.** "I tried X for 30 days" becomes your version, with your skills and your audience.
- **Same format, different topic.** A structure that worked in a neighbouring niche often works in yours.
- **Combine two.** Tick 2–4 saved outliers and click **Remix selected** for ideas that mix them.
- **Answer the comments.** Make the video the outlier's viewers asked for.

Then check it before you film: put the title in the [Title lab](../guides/scripts-and-titles.md), and pitch the idea to your [Audience lab](../guides/audience-lab.md) avatars.

## Underperformers teach too

A **0.3x** video on a channel that usually does well is useful too. The channel's audience was there, and they didn't click. Look at what's different from that channel's hits: usually it's the topic or the packaging. The **Analyze channel** button ranks a channel's videos from best to worst, so you can see both ends.
