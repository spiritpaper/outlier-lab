# Is the channel growing?

A good or bad week tells you almost nothing. Views swing with each upload, the season, the news and what YouTube tests on Home. To know whether a channel is really growing, look at longer blocks of time and at several numbers together.

## Your channel: the growth blocks

The AI review on **My channel** splits the last six months into **six 4-week blocks**. For each one it shows views, watch hours, net subscribers, impressions, click-through rate, and how many videos and Shorts you uploaded. You can see these numbers too: open **The data this review was based on** under a review.

How to read them:

- **Three or more blocks moving the same way** is a trend. One block up or down is usually noise or one video.
- **Views up, uploads flat**: real growth. Each video reaches more people.
- **Views up only in blocks with more uploads**: you're working harder, not growing. Check views per video.
- **Impressions up, CTR down**: YouTube is showing you to more people who don't know you yet. That's normal, and usually good (see [the CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)).
- **Impressions flat, CTR flat, views flat**: you're stuck at your current audience. You need ideas that reach further: look at outliers and content gaps.
- **Watch hours falling faster than views**: people are watching less of each video. Look at retention.

The review also tells you:

- **The share of views from non-subscribers.** Growth comes from people who don't know you yet. If almost all your views come from subscribers, YouTube isn't recommending you widely.
- **Views from new uploads vs your back catalog.** A healthy channel usually has older videos still bringing in views. If your old videos keep getting found, your channel has a base to build on.
- **Views by format.** Long-form, Shorts and live. YouTube runs separate recommendation systems for Shorts and long-form, so look at each on its own.

The AI is told to name what's holding growth back: reach (impressions), the click (CTR), retention, or upload pace.

## Other channels: what you can see

You don't have other people's analytics, but Outlier Lab tracks the channels you follow every day ([guide](../guides/channels.md)):

- **Trend** (growing, stable or declining): the median views of the channel's latest uploads against the ones before. Growing is 1.25x or more, declining is 0.8x or less.
- **% per month**: the long-run direction of their views per video.
- **Hit rate**: how many of their recent videos are 2x outliers. A high hit rate means they've found a format that keeps working.
- **Subscriber and median-view history**, and the **30-day change**.

**Compare** puts you next to them. Look for channels your size that are growing faster, then look at what they changed: new format, new series, new thumbnail style, more or fewer uploads.

## Things that look like growth but aren't

- **One viral video.** It lifts a whole month. Look at the median, not the total.
- **Seasonality.** Some niches peak at the same time every year (gift guides in December, tax videos in spring, school in September). Compare with the same block last year if you can.
- **The view counting change.** From 24 August 2026, YouTube counts a view from the first frame, including Home autoplay. Public view counts after that date can jump without anything else changing. YouTube says analytics, CTR, retention and recommendations stay on "engaged views", so My channel's numbers are not affected the same way.

## Posting more is not the same as growing

YouTube says to "prioritize consistent quality content over a high frequency of uploads", that taking a break isn't penalized, and that each video is judged on its own "rather than relying on past results". Paddy Galloway's advice to the channels he works with is the same: post less, but better. In his telling, Noah Kagan's channel went from three or four videos a week to three or four a month, and the first video after the change got 4.5 million views.

If your growth blocks show more uploads and the same views, you may get more from fewer, better-packaged videos.
