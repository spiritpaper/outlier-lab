# Reading retention curves

A retention curve shows, for every moment of a video, how many of the people who started it were still watching. It's the most honest number you have: it shows exactly where the video lost people.

In Outlier Lab, open **My channel** and tick up to 3 videos under **Audience retention** to overlay their curves. You need to be signed in with Google ([guide](../guides/my-channel.md)). For other people's videos you don't get their retention, but the **Most replayed** heatmap in the video panel shows which moments viewers rewatched.

## The shapes and what they mean

These are YouTube's own descriptions:

| Shape | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Gradual decline** | Normal. "Videos on YouTube generally taper off during the playback period." |
| **Flat stretch** | Viewers are watching that part from start to finish. |
| **Spike** | More viewers are "watching, rewatching, or sharing" that part. Above 100% means people went back to it. |
| **Dip** | Viewers "abandoning or skipping" at that moment. |

And some you'll see often:

- **A steep drop in the first 30 seconds.** Almost every video loses the most people at the start. A leaked MrBeast production memo says the same: "The first minute has the most loss" on nearly every video. The question is how steep it is compared with your other videos.
- **A cliff in the middle.** Something specific made people leave: a sponsor read, a long tangent, a recap of what they already know, or the moment the title's promise was paid off and there was nothing left to wait for.
- **A drop before the end.** Viewers leave when they sense the video is wrapping up ("so that's it for today…"). Keep the conclusion short, and point to the next video while they're still watching.

## The first 30 seconds

YouTube's **Intro** number is the percentage of viewers still watching after 30 seconds. YouTube says a high intro means "the content in the first 30 seconds matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title". Its advice when it's low:

- Change the thumbnail and title "to better reflect your video content".
- "Modify the first 30 seconds of your video and experiment with different styles".

For your next video, check the opening before you film: the **Script analyzer** looks for hook signals in your first 30 seconds (echoing the title, saying what the video will deliver, opening a question, raising stakes, talking to "you", getting to the point). See [Scripts, hooks and titles](../guides/scripts-and-titles.md).

## Compare like with like

- **Relative retention** (the second chart on My channel) compares each moment with YouTube videos of similar length: 0.5 is typical, 1.0 is the best. A 20-minute video will always have a lower raw curve than a 5-minute one, so use this one to compare videos of different lengths.
- **Typical retention** in YouTube Studio compares a video with your last 10 videos of similar length.
- **Your own videos** are the best benchmark. Overlay a video that did well with one that didn't, and look for where the lines split.

## Find the cause of a drop

1. **Note the timestamp** of the steepest drop. The summary on My channel gives it, with how many viewers are left at 10%, 50% and 90%.
2. **Watch that moment** of your video, starting 20 seconds before it. What changed? A new topic, a slow explanation, a sponsor, a promise paid off too early?
3. **Map it onto your script.** If you've saved the video's transcript, the **Script analyzer** shows your retention drop-offs on the script itself, and the AI channel review quotes what you said at the drop.
4. **Look for the same drop in other videos.** Paddy Galloway calls these "retention danger zones": points where your videos keep losing people. If it's the same spot every time (say, minute 2 when the intro ends), it's a habit you can fix.

## Fixes that come up again and again

These are rules of thumb from creators, not YouTube rules. Test them on your own channel.

- **Confirm the promise in the first seconds.** Show the thing from the thumbnail early.
- **Move your best moment earlier.** YouTube's own suggestion when your top moments come late: "Consider introducing the compelling content earlier in the video."
- **Re-hook when interest dips.** A new question, a twist, a tease of what's coming. The MrBeast memo plans a "re-engagement" around minute 3 and again around minute 6. Outlier Lab's script check marks **re-hook checkpoints** at 25%, 50% and 75%, and **drift zones**: a minute or more without a hook, twist, question or tease.
- **Cut the recap.** If you're repeating what viewers already saw, they'll skip it.
- **Expand on what people rewatch.** Spikes show what viewers liked most. YouTube suggests "creating newer content by expanding on the content from the top moments".
