# Hooks and keeping viewers

The title and thumbnail get the click. The video has to keep the viewer, and YouTube is watching: how long and how much of the video people watch is one of the signals it uses to decide who else to show it to.

To read your own curves, see [Reading retention curves](../data/retention.md). This page is about writing and editing the video so the curve stays high.

## The opening

**YouTube says:** its **Intro** number is how many viewers are still there after 30 seconds. A high intro means "the content in the first 30 seconds matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title". When it's low, YouTube suggests changing the title and thumbnail so they reflect the video better, or experimenting with the first 30 seconds.

**Creators say:** the MrBeast memo: "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video", and on nearly every video "the first minute has the most loss". The team puts more effort into it than any other part.

What a good opening does:

1. **Confirms the click.** Show or say what the title and thumbnail promised, right away. The viewer should know within seconds that they're in the right place.
2. **Raises a question** the rest of the video answers.
3. **Gives a reason to stay:** stakes, a twist, or a tease of the best moment.
4. **Gets to the point.** Skip the long greeting, the logo, the "before we start" and the channel update.

**Creators say:** Galloway's teams write **three or four versions of the intro**, record them, listen back and pick the best before shooting.

## The middle

Viewers drift. The fix is to give them new reasons to stay, regularly.

**Creators say** (MrBeast memo):

- **Minutes 1 to 3: stop telling, start showing.** "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." Move the story along quickly.
- **Minutes 3 to 6: the most exciting, simple content.** "If we can get them to watch the first half of the video there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end."
- **Re-engagements** around minute 3, and again around minute 6: a moment that renews interest.

Devices that win viewers back:

| Device | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Open loop / tease | "I'll show you the result at the end", "this is where it went wrong" |
| Twist | "but then", "it turns out" |
| Question | Ask what the viewer is wondering, then answer it |
| Stakes | What could go wrong, what it costs |
| Story beat | "That's when I realized…" |

**YouTube says:** if your strongest moments come late, "consider introducing the compelling content earlier in the video".

## The ending

- **Pay off the promise.** If the title asked a question, answer it clearly.
- **Don't announce the end.** "That's it for today" tells viewers to leave. Many creators end soon after the payoff.
- **Point to the next video** while people are still watching. YouTube recommends end screens, playlists and series to keep viewers watching.

## Length

There's no ideal length. YouTube's recommendation help page doesn't set one, and Test & compare picks the title or thumbnail that brings the most watch time, not the most clicks. A long video that holds people does well. A long video padded to be long does not. Make it as long as the idea needs, then cut what doesn't earn its place.
