# Ideas, titles and thumbnails

"Packaging" is what a viewer sees before they click: the title and the thumbnail. Together with the idea itself, it decides whether a video gets a chance at all. Paddy Galloway, who has worked with MrBeast, Ryan Trahan and Noah Kagan, estimates the click is roughly 40% of how a video performs, and says most creators give it far less effort than that.

## Start with the idea

**Creators say:** Galloway's view is that "the idea is more important than everything". Most creators spend about an hour a week on ideas, a tiny share of their time. His advice:

- **Have far more ideas than you make.** Brainstorm a lot, keep few.
- **Test each idea.** Does it excite you? Can you actually make it? Is there evidence it can reach your goal: one of your past videos, or a similar video that did well on another channel?
- **Ask who it's for.** A strong idea appeals to your core viewers *and* to casual and new ones.
- **Group your content into 3 or 4 buckets** (recurring series or themes), and brainstorm inside them.
- **Leave room for experiments** that have no evidence behind them yet.

And the hard rule: **"If we can't title and thumbnail an idea that can be clickable for our audience, it's not an idea anymore."** Write the title and sketch the thumbnail *before* you make the video.

## Titles

**Creators say** (Galloway):

- **Keep it simple and punchy.** Plain words that anyone can read, including viewers whose first language isn't yours.
- **Keep it short:** about 50 characters, 60 at most. Long titles get cut off on phones.
- **Write many, then narrow down** to the 4 or 5 best.

The MrBeast production memo says "Titles are equally as important for getting someone to click", and prefers intriguing, extreme wording: "I Survived" over "I Spent".

**What tends to work** (patterns that show up again and again in outlier titles):

- A number or a time span ("30 days", "$1 vs $1,000")
- An experiment or a challenge ("I tried…")
- A comparison or a contrast
- A question, or something that doesn't seem to add up, so the viewer wants the answer
- Stakes: money, danger, a mistake, a warning

**In Outlier Lab:** the [Title lab](../guides/scripts-and-titles.md) checks a title against a weighted checklist (fits on mobile, keyword early, curiosity or tension, at most one word in capitals, not repeating the thumbnail) and finds the proven outliers whose titles yours is closest to.

## Thumbnails

**Creators say** (Galloway):

- **What it shows matters more than the design.** "What is actually being shown in the thumbnail is so much more important than the design." Show a situation: action, danger, something unusual.
- **Few words, or none.** Under 5 or 6 words, often 2 to 4. Entertainment channels can often skip text, while education and commentary channels may need it.
- **Make really different concepts**, not the same picture with a different shirt colour. He sketches about 10 and narrows to 3.
- **The glance test:** a viewer should understand it "in just a glance of a second or two".
- **Get outside opinions** from other creators or your community before you choose.

**Title and thumbnail work as a pair.** The thumbnail should add something the title doesn't say, not repeat it. The Title lab's checklist flags a title that shares half its words with the thumbnail text.

**Be recognizable.** YouTube itself recommends "consistent titles and thumbnails", so regular viewers spot your videos at a glance.

**In Outlier Lab:** a project's **Thumbnail** section puts your thumbnail in a mock home feed next to your outliers, dark or light, full size and phone size. The AI can critique it against the outliers' thumbnails. See [Idea board and projects](../guides/idea-board.md).

## Honest packaging

The title and thumbnail are a promise. The MrBeast memo: "The title and thumbnail on the videos you will be producing set the expectations for the viewer for your video." If the video doesn't deliver, viewers leave, and YouTube notices.

YouTube's own description of what clickbait looks like in the numbers: a high CTR together with a low average view duration and few impressions. A click that disappoints is worse than no click.

## Test your packaging

**YouTube says:** **Test & compare** in YouTube Studio tests up to **three titles and thumbnails** on the same video, and picks the one with the most **watch time** (not the most clicks). It's for channels with access to advanced features, and not for Shorts. Results take from a few days to two weeks, and many tests end as "Performed same" or "Inconclusive". Then the first option you uploaded stays.

Because the winner is chosen by watch time, a test won't reward clickbait: a title that gets clicks and disappoints loses.

Without Test & compare, YouTube's CTR FAQ suggests comparing CTRs between videos over time, and not reading too much into small changes. Changing a title on an old video can help, but it's hard to tell whether the change or the timing made the difference.

**In Outlier Lab:** **Compare titles** in the Audience lab tests two titles on the same avatar audience before you publish.
