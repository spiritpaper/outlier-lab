# Myths

Things creators often believe about YouTube, and what YouTube has actually said about them.

## "There's a best time to post"

**YouTube says:** "publish time is not known to impact a video's long-term performance." For news, trends and anything with a short life, post as soon as it's ready. For evergreen videos, post whenever suits you: Todd Beaupré's reasoning is that if your audience doesn't care whether you uploaded at 3 in the morning or 7 at night, the algorithm shouldn't either. Over a few weeks, total views tend to even out whatever the upload time.

Timing still matters for Premieres and live streams, where people need to be there at the same time.

## "One bad video will kill my channel"

**YouTube says:** each video gets its own chance. "The algorithm uses fresh performance data for each individual video, rather than relying on past results." Todd Beaupré: "If your last video wasn't so great and your next video is great, we want to realize the potential of each video."

But a long run of videos your audience doesn't want "can, over time, affect your channel's overall performance". Experiment, but notice when an experiment isn't working.

## "Delete your flops (or your old viral video) to clean up the channel"

**YouTube says:** don't. Todd Beaupré: "Don't delete videos unless you have a very, very good reason. When you delete a video, you delete your channel's connection to the audience that watched that video." Rene Ritchie adds that making a video private also removes it from viewers' watch history, which can make them less likely to see your future videos.

If you must hide a video, make it **unlisted**, not deleted or private.

## "Taking a break gets you punished"

**YouTube says:** "the algorithm doesn't penalize creators for doing so!" It recommends telling your audience when you'll be away. It also says to "prioritize consistent quality content over a high frequency of uploads".

Views may dip while you're away, simply because there's no new video to recommend. That's not a penalty.

## "Shorts will hurt my long-form videos"

**YouTube says:** Shorts and long-form have separate recommendation systems, and "we haven't seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance."

**Creators say:** the real risk is the audience. Shorts on unrelated topics can bring subscribers who never watch your long videos. If your Shorts and long-form serve the same audience, keeping them on one channel helps (Todd Beaupré). See [Shorts](shorts.md).

## "Untick 'notify subscribers' to protect the video's performance"

**YouTube says:** most subscribers watch new uploads from Home anyway, and performance in one place mostly affects that same place. Rene Ritchie: "I wouldn't encourage anyone to uncheck it in hopes of improving performance for the algorithm." Only untick it when you deliberately want less visibility, for example when making a batch of old streams public at once.

## "Every subscriber sees every upload"

No. Subscribers aren't guaranteed to see or be notified of every upload, and Shorts don't send notifications at all. Most subscribers find your new videos on Home, which is personalized: each subscriber sees your video if YouTube thinks they'll want it.

## "A high CTR means a good video"

Not on its own. YouTube's CTR FAQ says CTR naturally falls as a video reaches more people (on Home, for example), and that a high CTR with a low average view duration and few impressions is what clickbait looks like. Test & compare picks the winning title or thumbnail by **watch time**, not clicks. Read CTR together with impressions and retention ([how](../data/diagnose-a-video.md)).

## "AI-made videos don't get recommended"

**YouTube says:** "Videos that are made with AI can be recommended as broadly as videos made without AI." They're judged like any other video: by how viewers respond.
